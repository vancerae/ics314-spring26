# Reflecting on the Use of AI in ICS 314

I. Introduction
Artificial Intelligence (AI) has become an increasingly influential tool in modern education, particularly in technical disciplines such as software engineering. In courses like ICS 314, where students are required to design, implement, debug, and document complex systems, AI tools can act as both assistants and accelerators of learning. These tools provide real-time feedback, generate code, explain concepts, and help troubleshoot issues, making them highly relevant in a fast-paced development environment.
Throughout this semester, I primarily used ChatGPT as my main AI tool. While other tools such as GitHub Copilot and Google Bard exist, my workflow centered on ChatGPT due to its ability to provide structured explanations, debugging assistance, and step-by-step guidance. In ICS 314, I used AI extensively while working with technologies such as Next.js, React, Prisma, PostgreSQL, and Bootstrap. These tools required both conceptual understanding and practical implementation, making AI support especially valuable.
This essay reflects on how AI influenced my learning, development process, and understanding of software engineering principles throughout the course.
II. Personal Experience with AI
Experience WODs (e.g., E18)
For experience WODs, I frequently used AI to help interpret instructions and generate starting points. A typical prompt I used was:
“Create a Next.js page that follows these WOD instructions…”
AI was extremely helpful in getting started quickly, especially when the instructions involved unfamiliar frameworks. However, the generated solutions were rarely perfect. I often had to modify imports, fix component structure, or adjust styling. The benefit was speed and direction, but the cost was time spent debugging AI-generated code.
In-class Practice WODs
During practice WODs, I used AI more cautiously. For example:
“Explain how to use React Bootstrap Navbar collapse properly”
This helped me understand concepts like Navbar.Toggle and Navbar.Collapse, which I later applied independently. AI was most useful here as a learning tool rather than a solution generator.
In-class WODs
For timed WODs, I relied less on AI because of time constraints and the need to demonstrate independent ability. However, I sometimes used it beforehand to prepare:
“Show a simple example of splitting a Next.js page into components”
This preparation improved my performance, but during the WOD itself, using AI was often impractical.
Essays
AI helped structure my essays and refine technical writing. Example prompt:
“Rewrite this reflection in a more technical software engineering tone”
The benefit was clarity and organization. The downside was ensuring the content still reflected my own voice and experience.
Final Project
During my final project (a Next.js + Prisma application), AI was heavily used for debugging and setup. Example prompts:
“Fix Prisma error: DATABASE_URL not set”
“Why is Next.js showing hydration mismatch in Safari?”
AI was extremely effective in diagnosing common issues such as environment configuration, Prisma migrations, and component rendering errors. However, it sometimes suggested outdated or incompatible fixes, requiring me to verify solutions.
Learning a Concept / Tutorial
AI was one of my primary tools for learning new concepts. Example:
“Explain Prisma migrations step by step”
This allowed me to quickly understand workflows like prisma migrate dev and database seeding. AI significantly accelerated conceptual learning.
Answering a Question in Class or Discord
I occasionally used AI to verify answers before posting:
“Is this explanation of server vs client components correct?”
This improved confidence but required careful validation to avoid spreading incorrect information.
Asking or Answering a Smart Question
AI helped refine questions:
“Turn this into a well-structured technical question”
This made my questions clearer and more effective when asking for help.
Coding Example
Example prompt:
“Give an example of mapping over an array in React to create components”
AI provided quick examples, which were useful as references.
Explaining Code
Example:
“Explain what this Next.js error means”
AI was highly effective here, often breaking down complex errors into understandable explanations.
Writing Code
AI was frequently used to generate boilerplate code:
“Create a React component with props and Bootstrap styling”
While useful, the generated code often required adjustments to fit project-specific requirements.
Documenting Code
Example:
“Write comments explaining this function”
AI helped improve readability and documentation quality.
Quality Assurance
Example:
“Fix the ESLint errors in this file”
AI was extremely helpful in resolving linting issues and improving code quality. However, it sometimes disabled rules instead of properly fixing issues, which required careful review.
Other Uses
AI was also used for debugging environment issues, such as:
Prisma client errors
Next.js module import issues
GitHub repository configuration
These were some of the most valuable uses of AI during the course.
III. Impact on Learning and Understanding
AI significantly enhanced my learning experience by providing immediate feedback and explanations. It allowed me to move past blockers quickly and focus on understanding concepts rather than being stuck on errors. However, it also introduced a risk of over-reliance. When AI provided complete solutions, I sometimes focused more on fixing code than fully understanding it.
Overall, AI improved my problem-solving efficiency but required conscious effort to ensure real learning was taking place.
IV. Practical Applications
Outside ICS 314, AI has practical applications in real-world development workflows. Tools like ChatGPT and Copilot are commonly used for debugging, documentation, and rapid prototyping. In collaborative environments, AI can accelerate development and reduce repetitive tasks.
In my own work, AI has already become a standard tool for troubleshooting and learning new technologies.
V. Challenges and Opportunities
One major challenge was that AI-generated solutions were not always correct or compatible with the current environment (e.g., Next.js version differences or Prisma configuration issues). This required additional debugging and validation.
Another challenge was balancing AI use with independent learning. It was easy to rely on AI instead of thinking through problems.
However, there are clear opportunities for integrating AI into education, such as guided debugging tools, AI-assisted code reviews, and interactive tutorials.
VI. Comparative Analysis
Traditional teaching methods emphasize problem-solving and independent thinking, while AI-enhanced approaches emphasize speed and accessibility.
AI improves:
Engagement through immediate feedback
Productivity in coding tasks
Accessibility of complex concepts
However, traditional methods are still stronger in:
Deep understanding
Long-term retention
Independent problem-solving skills
The most effective approach is a combination of both.
VII. Future Considerations
AI will likely play an even larger role in software engineering education. Future improvements may include:
More accurate and context-aware code generation
Better integration with development environments
AI tools tailored specifically for education
However, it will be important to ensure students still develop core problem-solving skills.
VIII. Conclusion
AI played a significant role in my experience in ICS 314, acting as both a learning tool and a development assistant. It helped me debug complex issues, learn new technologies, and complete assignments more efficiently. However, it also required careful use to avoid over-reliance and ensure genuine understanding.
Moving forward, AI should be integrated thoughtfully into software engineering education, balancing its strengths with traditional learning methods. When used correctly, AI is not a replacement for learning but a powerful tool that enhances it.
